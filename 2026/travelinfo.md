# International Travelers Guide

Welcome to Sabah, one of the states of East Malaysia, with its name - The Land Below the Wind thanks to its relatively calm tropical climate rainforests, diverse wildlife, mountain landscapes and remote islands.

This guide consists a mix of official tourism resources, travel guides and mostly experiences from locals and international visitors who have explored Sabah.
Unlike in major urban areas, getting around here generally involves some form of travelling or longer journeys to get around.

While we aim to keep things accurate and practical, travel conditions, transportation options and accessibility may vary depending on the season - so we recommend only using this as a starting point and take some extra care in the rest of your plannings.

## Entry/Visa Requirements

### WEST MALAYSIANS / LOCALS

---

- Local Malaysians (departing from West Malaysia) do not need a visa to enter Sabah/Sarawak. However, one must go through the routine of immigration checks on arrival or departure.
- This checking applies to all statewide airports in Sabah/Sarawak Airports - Kota Kinabalu (BKI), Sandakan (SDK), Kuching (KCH), Sibu (SBW), etc.
- MyKad / MyKid (NRIC) is **REQUIRED** for the checking. Passport is **OPTIONAL** but also accepted.
- The Immigration Officer will issue a **Journey/Visit Pass Slip** granting up to **90 DAYS** of stay in the states.
- However, please do note that this pass is **NOT** a visa, it is just an official record of your entry. Please keep the slip as it will be **REQUIRED** when exiting the state.

### FOREIGN NATIONALS

---

#### Visa / Entry Requirements for Malaysia - FOREIGN NATIONALS

- To check the eligible countries, whether under **A. Visa-Exempt** or **B. Visa-Required**, please check the following: <br><https://embassymalaysia.nl/list-of-countries-that-need-a-visa-to-enter-malaysia/>

##### A. Visa-Exempt Countries

- Most foreign nationals can visit Malaysia for short-term visits purposes (tourism, business, etc).
- For many visa-exempt passports, the general duration of stay is up to 90 days.

###### 1. Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) Member States

- This section is applicable for the following ASEAN Members which includes:
  - Brunei Darussalam
  - Cambodia
  - Indonesia
  - Laos
  - Myanmar
  - Philippines
  - Singapore
  - Thailand
  - Vietnam
- Visa-free entry is allowed for 30 days, with some exceptions for Myanmar (14 days).
- For duration of stay exceeding of ONE (1) month, visa is required except for Brunei and Singapore nationals.

###### 2. European Union (EU) Member States

- Citizens from EU Member States, visa-free entry of up to 90 days is typically granted under standard visa-exemption policy.

###### 3. Temporary Diplomatic Measures

- India and China passport holders are also eligible for visa-free short term visits until at least 31 December 2026.

#### B. Visa-Required / eVISA / Entry With Visa

- Nationals of certain countries must obtain a visa before visiting Malaysia.
- For eligible travellers from visa-required countries, a visa can be applied via the online eVISA / MYVISA Portal. Subsequently, it can also be applied at Malaysia Embassies/Consulates abroad (subject to availability or eligibility).
- Some countries may only allow visas if the mode of entry is by air.
- For travellers entering from certain countries with health risks, a valid Yellow Fever Vaccination Certificate may be required.

### Passport Validity, MDAC and Travel Documentations

- ==**Passports** must be valid for at least **SIX (6) MONTHS** on arrival.==
- ALL travellers are required to complete the **Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)** - except Singapore Citizens, which can be done online before arrival (within 3 days prior).<br><https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main>

## Customs Declarations

1. ALL dutiable or prohibited goods arriving or leaving Malaysia must be declared at all entry and exit points.
2. Regarding **PORK PRODUCTS**, you are **NOT ALLOWED** to bring in or out of the respective state due to disease control concerns.
3. Due to The Environmental Protection Laws, any form of **Fresh Produce are NOT ALLOWED** to be brought into the state.
4. You are **NOT ALLOWED** to bring Ringgit Malaysia (RM) **exceeding USD 10,000 cash** or **Bearer Negotiable Instruments (CBNI) equivalent** in or out of Malaysia, without written approval from Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM).
   Any amount in excess above the limit shall be deemed prohibited.
5. Failure to make a declaration as mentioned above is considered an offense. Upon conviction, one may be fined up to RM3 million or imprisoned for a term up to 5 years, or both.

## Logistics and Travel Information

### 1. Transit via KLIA T1/T2

- **KLIA T1 -** used for Malaysia Airlines (MH) and Firefly (FY) Flights
- **KLIA T2 -** used for Airasia (AK) Flights, and other Low-Cost Airlines
- When transiting via the same airline, typically you would not be required to collect your luggage. Feel free to head straight to the Transit or Domestic Transfer area for your next flight.
- You may be required to re-clear immigration and perform a security checking, based on local procedures from time to time.
- However, for different airlines, in most cases, you would need to re-collect your baggages, head to the correct terminal and re-check in them accordingly.
- Make sure to also allow sufficient transit time in the event of a flight reschedule or delay.

### 2. Travel Reminders

## Payment Information

### 1. Cash Payments and ATMs in Malaysia

- Cash remains widely accepted across Malaysia, especially at smaller shops, hawker stalls and night markets. ATMs are also available in malls, petrol stations or nearby banks, where most machines generally accept Visa, MasterCard or UnionPay, with some that might support International Cards like AMEX and JCB.
- When using foreign cards or banks than your own, please be aware of any possible withdrawal fees or card-network switching fees.
- ATMs generally dispense RM50 or RM100 denominations, and might have varying withdrawal limits between banks.

### 2. E-Wallets and Cashless Options

- The most common local e-Wallets include **Touch 'n Go eWallet** as well as **Boost**, which are widely accepted in retail stores, parking lots and food stalls.
- GrabPay and ShopeePay are also commonly used, but it is mainly available for restaurants, cafes or supermarkets.
- Foreigners may enjoy using these e-Wallets, but some may require a Malaysian Bank Account to perform any form of credit top-ups.
- Most major shops, supermarkets also accept debit/credit cards or any payment channels that is compatible with the DuitNow QR (Malaysia's unified QR Payment System)
