# International Travelers Guide

Welcome to Sabah, one of the states of the East Malaysia, with its name - The Land Below the Wind thanks to its relatively calm tropical climate with rainforests, diverse wildlife,
mountain landscapes and remote islands.

This guide consists a mix of official tourism resources, travel guides and mostly experiences from locals and international visitors who have explored Sabah.
Unlike its counterpart in major urban areas, getting around here would involve some form of travelling or longer journeys to get around.

While we aim to keep things accurate and pratical, travel conditions, transportation options and accessibility may vary depending on seasons - so we recommend using this as a starting point with some extra planning.

## Entry/Visa Requirements

### WEST MALAYSIANS / LOCALS

---

- Local Malaysians (departing from West Malaysia) do not need a visa to enter Sabah/Sarawak. However, must go through immigration checks.
- *Applies to ALL Sabah/Sarawak Airports - Kota Kinabalu (BKI), Sandakan (SDK), Kuching (KCH), Sibu (SBW) etc
- MyKad / MyKid (NRIC) would be required for the checking. Passport is optional but also accepted.
- Immigration Officer will issue a "Journey/Visit Pass Slip" granting up fo 90 days stay in the states.
- *This Pass is not a visa, it is just an official record of your entry. Please keep the slip as it will be required when exiting the state.

### FOREIGN NATIONALS

---

#### Visa / Entry Requirements for Malaysia - FOREIGN NATIONALS

- To check for the eligible countries, whether under **A. Visa-Exempt** or **B. Visa-Required**, please check the following: <br><https://embassymalaysia.nl/list-of-countries-that-need-a-visa-to-enter-malaysia/>

##### A. Visa-Exempt Countries

- Most foreign nationals can visit Malaysia for short-term visits purposes (tourism, business and etc).
- For many visa-exempt passports, the typical allowed stay is up to 90 days.

###### 1. Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) Member States

- This section is applicable for the following ASEAN Members which includes:
  - Brunei Darussalam
  - Cambodia
  - Indonesia
  - Laos
  - Myanmar
  - Philippines
  - Singapore
  - Thailand
  - Vietnam
- Visa-free entry is allowed for 30 days, with some exceptions for Myanmar (14 days).
- For duration of stay exceeding of ONE (1) month, visa is required except for Brunei and Singapore nationals.

###### 2. European Union (EU) Member States

- Citizens from EU Member States, visa-free entry of up to 90 days is typically granted under standard visa-exemption policy.

###### 3. Temporary Diplomatic Measures

- India and China Passport Holders are also eligible for visa-free short term visits until at least 31 December 2026.

#### B. Visa-Required / eVISA / Entry With Visa

- Nationals of certain countries must obtain a visa before visiting Malaysia.
- For eligible travellers from visa-required countries, a visa can be applied via the online eVISA / MYVISA Portal.
- Subsequently, it can also be applied at Malaysia Embassies/Consulates abroad (subject to availability or eligibility).
- *Some of the countries may only allow visas if the mode of entry is by air.
- For travellers incoming from certain countries with health risks, a valid Yellow Fever Vaccination Certificate may be required.

### Passport Validity, MDAC and Travel Documentations

- ==**Passports** must ba valid for at least **SIX (6) months** on arrival.==
- **ALL travellers are to complete the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)** - except Singapore Citizens, online before arrival (within 3 days prior).<br><https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main>

## Customs Declarations

1. ALL dutiable or prohibited goods arriving or leaving Malaysia must be declared at all entry and exit points.
2. Regarding **PORK PRODUCTS**, you are **NOT ALLOWED** to bring into or out of within the respective state due to disease control concerns.
3. Due to Environmental Protection Laws, any form of **Fresh Produces (Fruits and Vegetables) are NOT ALLOWED** to be brought into the state.
4. You are **NOT ALLOWED** to bring into or out of Malaysia, Ringgit Malaysia (RM) **exceeding USD 10,000 Cash** or **Bearer Negotiable Instruments (CBNI) equivalent**, unless with written approval from Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM).
   Any amount in excess above the limit shall be deemed prohibited.
5. Failure to make a declaration if any of the above is met is considered an offense, upon conviction, may be fined not exceeding RM3 Million or imprisoned for a term not exceeding 5 years, or both.

## Logistics and Travel Information

### 1. Transit via KLIA T1/T2

- **KLIA T1 -** used for Malaysia Airlines (MH) and Firefly (FY) Flights
- **KLIA T2 -** used for Airasia (AK) Flights, and other Low-Cost Airlines
- When transit via the same airline, typically you would not need to collect your luggage. Feel free to directly head straight to the Transit or Domestic Transfer area.
- You may be required to clear immigration and perform a security checking, based on local procedures.
- However, for different airlines, in most cases, you would need to re-collect your baggages, head to the correct terminal and re-check in them accordingly.
- Make sure to also allow enough transit time, minimum 2-3 hours recommended in the event of a flight-retime or delays.

### 2. Travel Reminders

<span style="color:#FFFBE8;">Please refrain from bringing in any form of **FRESH PRODUCE** (Vegetables and Fruits) or **ANIMAL PRODUCTS / FOOD WASTE** (Frozen Delicacies or Canned Animal Products). You will be either required to declare them or bin them.</span>
:::
- Your Destination Airport would be - **Kota Kinabalu International Airport (KKIA) [IATA - BKI, ICAO - WBKK)**
- The state operates at the **GMT+8 Timezone** so please be advised to time your clocks as flight times will be based on the aforementioned timezone.

- Power Plugs and Sockets are of Type-G, and running on 240V at a frequency of 50 Hz.

## Payment Information

### 1. Cash Payments and ATMs in Malaysia

- Cash remains widely accepted across Malaysia, especially at smaller shops, hawker stalls and night markets. ATMs are also available in malls, petrol stations or nearby banks, where most machinese generally accept Visa, MasterCard or UnionPay, with some that might support International Cards like AMEX and JCB.
- When using foreign cards or other banks than your own card carrier, please be aware of possible withdrawal fees or card-network switching fees.
- ATMs generally dispense RM50 or RM100 denominations, and might have varying withdrawal limits between banks.

### 2. E-Wallets and Cashless Options

- The most common local e-Wallets include **Touch 'n Go eWallet** as well as **Boost**, which widely accepted in retail stores, parkings and food stalls.
- GrabPay and ShopeePay are also commonly used, but mainly for restaurants, cafes or supermarkets.
- Foreigners may enjoy using these e-wallets, but some may require a Malaysian Bank Account for top-ups.
- Most major shops, supermarkets also accept debit/credit cards or any payment channels that is compatible with the DuitNow QR (Malaysia's unified QR Payment System)
